Introduction

The second trimester (Weeks 13–27) is often called the "golden phase" of pregnancy. Morning sickness usually eases, energy levels improve, and your growing baby becomes more active. While this stage is generally more comfortable, it's also a time when your baby's development accelerates and your body undergoes important changes.

Here's what you can expect—and how to prepare for the months ahead.

What Happens During the Second Trimester?

As your baby grows rapidly during the second trimester, you'll likely notice a more visible baby bump and begin feeling those exciting first movements, often called quickening, between 18 and 22 weeks.

Many women also experience increased energy, a healthier appetite, and steady weight gain as the pregnancy progresses. Mild backaches, round ligament pain, occasional swelling in the feet and ankles, and skin changes such as darkening around the nipples or patches on the face (melasma) are also common. Remember, every pregnancy is different, so your experience may not be exactly the same as someone else's.

When should you consult a doctor?

Regular prenatal appointments become even more important during the second trimester.

Your doctor may recommend:

  • Anomaly Scan (18–22 weeks) to evaluate your baby's growth and organ development.
  • Blood pressure monitoring.
  • Blood tests if required.
  • Screening for gestational diabetes (typically between 24–28 weeks).
  • Monitoring your baby's heartbeat and growth.

NEVER skip scheduled appointments, even if you feel perfectly healthy.

Looking Ahead

The second trimester is also a great time to begin preparing for your baby's arrival. Start exploring childbirth classes, planning your maternity essentials, and discussing your birth preferences with your healthcare provider.

Remember: Every pregnancy is different. Stay connected with your doctor, listen to your body, and enjoy this exciting stage as your little one continues to grow.
