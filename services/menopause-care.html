FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

1. When does menopause usually begin?
Most women experience menopause between the ages of 45 and 55, although the timing can vary. The transition leading up to menopause, known as perimenopause, may begin several years earlier.
2. What symptoms are common during menopause?
Symptoms may vary however commonly women face symptoms like hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disturbances, mood changes, vaginal dryness, weight changes, and fatigue. Every woman’s experience is unique, and symptoms can vary in intensity.
3. Are all menopausal symptoms treated with hormone therapy?
Not necessarily. Depending on your symptoms, medical history, and preferences, your doctor may recommend hormonal treatment, non-hormonal options, or lifestyle modifications.
4. Why is bone health important after menopause?
As oestrogen levels decline, the risk of reduced bone density increases. Regular assessments and preventive care can help maintain bone strength and reduce the risk of osteoporosis.
5. Can lifestyle changes help manage menopause?
Yes. A balanced diet, regular physical activity, good sleep habits, and stress management can play a meaningful role in improving symptoms and supporting long-term health.
